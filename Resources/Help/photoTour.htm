Photo Tour Tab

The Photo Tour Tab configures automated, multi-leg visual discovery flights. In a Photo Tour scenario, you depart from an airfield, navigate visually through a series of geographic waypoints where real-world photos were taken and shared to Pic2Map, and conclude with a landing at a destination airport.

Design Philosophy: Photo Tours are designed around low-and-slow visual flight rules ("IFR – I Follow Roads"). As you navigate from one photo coordinate to the next using terrain features, highways, and the moving map overlay, entering each location's trigger zone sounds an audio chime, displays the photo taken at that spot, and advances the moving map to the next leg.

How Photo Tours Are Built

Because Photo Tours construct an interconnected journey around real-world geolocated images, airfield selection is automated algorithmically rather than chosen manually on the General tab. However, both the departure and destination airports are strictly constrained by your active Location Filter:

  1. Seed Photo & Departure Airfield: The generator queries Pic2Map for a random geolocated photo. It then searches the airport database for a valid departure runway located within your configured Min Leg Dist and Max Leg Dist from that photo that also matches your active Location Filter (Country, State, City).
  2. Leg Chaining: From the current photo, the generator inspects adjacent photo candidates, selecting an unvisited photo that satisfies distance bounds and your Max Bearing turn limits.
  3. Tour Length Verification: Chaining continues until the tour reaches a leg count between Min No. Legs and Max No. Legs.
  4. Destination Airfield: Once the final photo waypoint is established, the generator searches for a suitable arrival airfield within leg distance range that satisfies your active Location Filter.
  5. Asset Compilation: All waypoint photos are downloaded and automatically resized to fit your configured photo display window. Route charts showing the active leg line over OpenStreetMap (OSM) tiles and mission scripts are compiled into the flight package.

Photo Tour Parameters

The Photo Tour Parameters group defines the geometric constraints, distance thresholds, and search budgets used by the route-finding algorithm.

Min Leg Dist & Max Leg Dist text boxes

Specifies the minimum and maximum acceptable distance (in nautical miles) for each individual leg of the tour (defaults: 3 nm minimum, 10 nm maximum).

  • Applies to the initial hop from the departure runway to the first photo, all intermediate legs between photos, and the final hop from the last photo to the destination airport.
  • Validation: The minimum leg distance must be strictly less than the maximum leg distance. Shorter distances produce compact tours ideal for light aircraft; larger values allow faster aircraft to cover broader regions.

Min No. Legs & Max No. Legs text boxes

Defines the lower and upper bounds for the total number of legs in the generated tour (defaults: 2 min, 7 max).

  • The total leg count includes the departure leg, intermediate photo hops, and the landing approach leg. For example, a setting of 2 legs produces a single-photo tour: Departure Airport → Photo → Destination Airport.
  • Validation: Minimum legs cannot exceed maximum legs. Setting a higher minimum creates longer flights with more photo waypoints, but may require more search attempts in sparsely photographed regions.

Max Bearing text box

Sets the maximum allowable course change (in degrees) between consecutive legs (default: 135°).

  • This constraint prevents multi-leg tours from doubling back or producing erratic hairpin reversals, ensuring a natural, forward-progressing cross-country flight path.
  • Two-Leg Exemption: If the tour consists of exactly 2 legs (a single photo visit between departure and arrival), the maximum bearing limit is not applied. This bypass makes it significantly easier to generate single-photo flights where the inbound and outbound headings may require a sharp turn or direct return.

Hotspot Radius text box

Specifies the detection radius (in meters) of the three-dimensional cylinder trigger placed over each photo waypoint (default: 1000 meters).

  • When your aircraft penetrates this cylinder, the simulator triggers an audio chime (ThruHoop.wav), opens the photo viewer window, and advances the scenario leg counter (currentLegNo).
  • Tighter radii (e.g., 500 m) demand precise visual navigation directly over the photo site. Larger radii (e.g., 1500–2000 m) offer a more relaxed touring experience.

Max Attempts text box

Sets the maximum number of full search attempts the generator will make before stopping if a valid sequence of photos cannot be resolved (default: 100).

If the algorithm picks a seed photo in a remote area without nearby matching airports or adjacent photos meeting your distance/bearing constraints, it discards that chain and retries with a fresh seed photo. Increasing this value gives the generator more opportunities to find tours in challenging regions or restrictive location filters.

Photo Window Location

Configures the monitor index, screen alignment, and margin offsets for the in-game photo viewer window when triggered during flight. For a detailed explanation of multi-monitor indexing, window alignment modes, and pixel offset calculations, see the Settings Tab: Map Window Settings documentation.

Note: The settings in this group govern only the pop-up photo viewer window. Display positioning and dimensions for the moving map overlay window are configured independently on the Settings Tab.

Automated Image Dimension Scaling

During scenario generation, each downloaded photo is analyzed against your target monitor dimensions and margin offsets:

  • If a photo's raw pixel dimensions exceed the safe viewing boundaries (calculated from monitor size minus margins and offsets), the generator automatically downscales the image while strictly preserving its original aspect ratio.
  • This ensures that photo popups never overlap multiple displays, clip outside screen edges, or exceed your intended panel real estate.

In-Flight Operations & Procedures

Once generated, the scenario loads directly into Prepar3D with automated event triggers, custom audio, and interactive tracking.

Pre-Flight & Departure

  1. Initial Position: Your aircraft is positioned cold and dark at the departure runway threshold with the simulator paused.
  2. Briefing Audio: Unpausing the simulation triggers spoken text-to-speech audio introducing your flight, followed by the automatic opening of the in-game Moving Map overlay.
  3. Moving Map Display: The map display opens centered on the first leg, showing your aircraft's spatial position and a line drawn from your departure airport to the first photo waypoint. For detailed instructions on map viewport panning, zoom cycling, and hardware switch bindings, see Settings Tab: Moving Map Display & Controls.

En Route Navigation & Waypoint Arrival

  • Visual Tracking: Fly toward the destination of the active leg following roads, terrain landmarks, and the route line drawn across the OpenStreetMap chart.
  • Triggering Waypoints: When your aircraft enters the waypoint's hotspot cylinder, you will hear a confirmation chime (ThruHoop.wav).
  • Viewing the Photo: The photo window opens automatically displaying the image taken at that coordinate. When your moving map and photo window are configured to use different screen alignments (e.g., Top Left for map, Top Right for photos) or separate monitors, both windows display cleanly side-by-side without overlapping.
  • Automatic Leg Progression: As the waypoint triggers, the moving map automatically updates its route line to guide you along the next leg toward the subsequent photo or destination airfield.

Final Leg & Mission Completion

After reaching the final photo waypoint, the moving map draws a line directly toward your destination airport. Land on any active runway at the airfield and bring your aircraft to a complete stop to resolve the mission objective (Goal01) and close all active overlays.

Global Actions

The bottom buttons (Reset Tab, Reload Profile, and Generate Scenario) are persistent controls documented under General Tab: Global Actions.

When generating a Photo Tour scenario, the following tab-specific validations must also pass:

  • Min Leg Dist must be strictly less than Max Leg Dist.
  • Min No. Legs must be less than or equal to Max No. Legs.
  • A valid RapidAPI key must be configured on the Settings Tab to download OpenStreetMap route tiles.
